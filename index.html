How to read a listedeville

Twelve lists, seven occasions, one filter away.

Every city is sorted into the same twelve lists — the restaurants and bars, of course, but also the speakeasies you need a password for, the rooftops worth the elevator, the coffee, the brunch, the bakeries, the dessert, the scene, the nightlife, and an off-beat list for the things that could only exist in that town. Think of them as twelve short books on one shelf.

Then every place is tagged for the evening you're actually planning: romantic, date night, casual, group, brunch, late night, sweets. Tap one on a city page and the whole guide narrows to fit — add a price band, a neighborhood word in the search box, or "Michelin-starred," and you're down to a shortlist you can book from. The little marks beside each name (★, Bib, 50 Best, NYT, JBF) say who else agrees; the Amex tick says the dinner pays you back.

The compiler

Tony Abraham

Technology entrepreneur. New Yorker. A passionate home cook who learned that the best way to understand a dish is to try to recreate it the next morning.

Honestly Speaking is written from a simple conviction: that the food itself — its taste, its texture, its quiet decisions — deserves a more careful kind of attention than it usually gets. The plate, not the room. The dish, not the hype.

Every guide is cross-referenced against the Michelin Guide, the World's 50 Best, The New York Times, The Infatuation, Eater, Resy, OpenTable and Yelp — then argued down to the places worth an evening.
