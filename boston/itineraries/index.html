Boston · for a first-time visitor

Just follow this.

Three ready-made food itineraries — one, three and five days — built only from places on the list, so you can eat well without doing the research. Every place links back to the guide.

Itinerary

One day

North End to the South End, on foot. The day that shows why Boston argues about cannoli.

You have one day. Everything is walkable or one T stop; book one dinner. Book one thing: dinner at Toro (Resy) or Pammy's (Resy, across the river).

The day

  1. 8:00Flour (Fort Point or Back Bay)Joanne Chang's sticky bun and a coffee. Walk-in; the Fort Point original is the one.
  2. 9:30North EndCaffè Vittoria for an espresso under the 1929 tin ceiling, then the Paul Revere House and Old North Church before the tour groups.
  3. 12:00Galleria Umberto (North End)Sicilian squares and arancini, cash only, until they sell out around one. Then the cannoli: Modern or Mike's — pick a side, take it to the waterfront.
  4. 2:00Neptune Oyster (North End)The hot buttered lobster roll at the marble bar. Walk-in only; the wait is real, put your name down and wander.
  5. 4:00The Wig Shop (Downtown Crossing)Through the wig shop. One cocktail, then walk the Common to Beacon Hill.
  6. 6:00Hecate (Back Bay)Beneath Krasi — 50 Best entry, Greek wine and cocktails. Resy.
  7. 7:30Toro (South End)Ken Oringer and Jamie Bissonnette's tapas room, still the loudest and best in the South End. Resy. Backup: Coppa two blocks over (walk-in) or Bar Mezzana (Resy).
  8. 10:00Wally's Cafe (South End)Jazz since 1947, a few dollars at the door, cash at the bar.

Itinerary

Three days

The city, then Cambridge and Somerville, then the Seaport and Southie. One side of the river a day.

A long weekend. Day one is the one-day plan; day two crosses the Charles; day three ends on the water. Book three dinners the day reservations open: Toro, Pammy's, and O Ya or Fox & the Knife.

Day 1 · The city

  1. All dayFollow the one-day planFlour, the North End, Neptune, the Wig Shop, Hecate, Toro, Wally's.

Day 2 · Cambridge & Somerville

  1. 9:00Sofra (Watertown line)Maura Kilpatrick's Turkish pastries and a Turkish coffee. Walk-in; the morning bun sells out.
  2. 11:00Harvard SquareMr. Bartley's for a burger if you're hungry already; L.A. Burdick for drinking chocolate if not.
  3. 1:00Pagu (Central Square)Tracy Chang's Japanese-Spanish lunch — a Bib. Resy. Or Jahunger (Uyghur, Bib) if you want the surprise.
  4. 3:00Toscanini's (Central Square)Burnt caramel. The best ice cream in the city, and it's not close.
  5. 5:00Bow Market (Union Square, Somerville)Remnant Brewing in the courtyard, the shops around it. Then Backbar, through the hallway.
  6. 7:30Pammy's (Cambridge)The room Michelin called 'recommended' and everyone else calls the best in Cambridge. Resy, 30 days. Backup: Giulia (Resy) or Sarma (Resy, meze in Somerville).
  7. 10:00The Sinclair or Lizard LoungeA show, if there is one; otherwise Charlie's Kitchen upstairs for the jukebox.

Day 3 · Seaport & Southie

  1. 9:00Lincoln Tavern (South Boston)The Southie brunch. Resy, or a wait.
  2. 11:00Castle IslandWalk the loop, then Sullivan's for a hot dog and a soft serve — a James Beard America's Classic.
  3. 1:30James Hook & Co (Seaport)A lobster roll on the pier.
  4. 3:00Trillium Fort PointThe rooftop, a beer, the harbor.
  5. 5:00Drink (Fort Point)No menu; tell them what you like. Walk-in; go early.
  6. 7:30O Ya (Leather District) or Fox & the Knife (South Boston)O Ya for Tim Cushman's omakase (Tock; the splurge); Fox & the Knife for Karen Akunowicz's Bib-winning pasta (Resy). Backup: Row 34 (Resy).
  7. 10:00Lookout Rooftop (The Envoy)The skyline across the channel, one last drink.

Itinerary

Five days

The three-day plan, plus the North Shore clam trail and one day for the counters.

Enough time to leave the city: fried clams in Essex, roast beef on Revere Beach, and the tasting counters back in town. Set alarms for 311 Omakase (Tock), O Ya (Tock), Toro and Pammy's.

Days 1–3

  1. AllFollow the three-day planThe city, Cambridge and Somerville, the Seaport and Southie.

Day 4 · The North Shore

  1. 9:00Kane's Donuts (Saugus)The honey dip, on the way north. Cash-friendly, walk-in.
  2. 11:00Woodman's of EssexWhere the fried clam was invented in 1916. Whole-belly, with the tartar. Walk-in; the line is the ritual.
  3. 1:30RockportRoy Moore Lobster Co on Bearskin Neck — a lobster on the back deck. Cash.
  4. 4:00Kelly's Roast Beef (Revere Beach)On the way back: the roast beef sandwich that started the North Shore's rivalry, eaten on the seawall.
  5. 6:00Santarpio's (East Boston)Pizza since 1903, lamb tips from the grill, cash only. Walk-in.
  6. 8:00Kowloon (Saugus)The Route 1 tiki palace. Scorpion bowls. You've earned it.

Day 5 · The counters

  1. 10:00The Paramount (Beacon Hill)The line, the pancakes. Cash-friendly, walk-in.
  2. 12:00Boston Public Market → HaymarketThe market hall, then the Friday–Saturday produce market that's been shouting since 1830.
  3. 2:00Union Oyster HouseOysters at the bar where Daniel Webster ate — America's oldest restaurant, 1826. Walk-in at the bar.
  4. 4:00Contessa (The Newbury)One drink under the glass roof. Resy.
  5. 6:00Offsuit (Leather District)Behind Troquet, reservation only, the quietest good bar in the city.
  6. 8:00311 Omakase ★ (South End)Boston's first Michelin star: Wei Fa Chen's counter in a rowhouse. Tock, weeks out. Alternative: Uni at the Eliot (Resy; Ken Oringer's sashimi bar) or Mooncusser's seafood tasting (Resy).
  7. 11:00Bova's (North End)Open 24 hours. A cannoli for the road.

Gotchas & reminders

Before you go.

The things New Yorkers know and visitors learn the expensive way.

Reservations, and the walk-in bars.

Resy rooms release 30 days out at 9 or 10am; Tock counters (311 Omakase, O Ya, Tasting Counter) in blocks. Neptune, Galleria Umberto, Drink and the North End caffès don't take reservations — arrive early or accept the wait.

Cash is the North End's language.

Galleria Umberto, Santarpio's, Kelly's, Roy Moore, Wally's and most of Haymarket are cash-only or cash-first. Modern and Mike's take cards but the line moves faster with bills.

Tip 20%; tax is 7%.

Massachusetts meals tax is 6.25% plus a local 0.75% in Boston; menus exclude it. Tip 18–22% on the pre-tax total. Some restaurants add a kitchen appreciation fee — read the bill.

Last call is 2am, and the T stops before that.

Bars close at 2am (some at 1am). The T's last trains leave downtown around 12:30am — after that it's a car. Cambridge and Somerville are a short, cheap ride.

Winter is real; so is patio season.

Rooftops and beer gardens run May through October; the Envoy's Lookout does winter igloos. Fried-clam shacks on the North Shore close for the season — Woodman's stays open, the Clam Box doesn't always.

Sunday and Monday closures.

Many chef-owned rooms close one or both — Pammy's, Sarma, Juliet, Oleana all do at least one. The North End runs seven days.

The cannoli question.

Mike's is the famous one; Modern is the one locals send you to; Maria's makes the best sfogliatelle. Bova's is open at 2am. The correct answer is to try two.

Fried clams: whole-belly, or don't.

Strips are for people who don't like clams. Order whole-belly, with tartar, and expect to pay $30+ — clam prices have doubled since 2020.

Use the Amex credit like a local.

Platinum $100 per quarter, Gold $50 twice a year, at any Resy or Tock venue showing the 'Resy Credit eligible' badge — no booking through Resy required.

Anything marked verify, verify.

Boston lost a run of beloved rooms — the Middle East, Great Scott, Doyle's, Craigie, Island Creek Kenmore. A minute on Resy or Google before you cross the river.

Roast beef: the three-way.

On the North Shore a 'three-way' is roast beef with barbecue sauce, mayo and American cheese on an onion roll. Kelly's is the original; Nick's and Bill & Bob's are the fights.

One for the road.

Logan's Terminal B has a Legal Sea Foods and Terminal C a Dunkin'. South Station has Pavement and, for the train, a Flour box across the street.
