Washington, DC · for a first-time visitor

Just follow this.

Three ready-made food itineraries — one, three and five days — built only from places on the list, so you can eat well without doing the research. Every place links back to the guide.

Itinerary

One day

The Mall in the morning, Shaw and 14th Street after dark. Everything by foot and Metro.

You have one day between meetings, or one night before the train. Book one dinner; walk into everything else. Book one thing: dinner at Albi (Resy, 30 days) — or The Dabney if Albi is gone.

The day

  1. 8:00Yellow (Georgetown or Navy Yard)The pistachio sticky bun and a coffee, then a pita breakfast sandwich if you're staying a while. Walk-in; the line moves.
  2. 9:30The MallWalk the Monument to the Lincoln Memorial before the school groups arrive. The museums are free; the cafés inside are on this list.
  3. 12:30Sweet Home Café (NMAAHC)The best museum lunch in America — Carolina gold rice, smoked catfish, the fried chicken. Timed-entry passes for the museum are free but book online.
  4. 2:30Ben's Chili Bowl (U Street)A half-smoke, all the way, at the counter where Obama ate. Cash is fastest. Then walk Shaw's murals down to Blagden Alley.
  5. 4:30Calico or Dabney Cellar (Blagden Alley)A glass in the alley before dinner — Calico for the backyard, the Cellar for the wine list under The Dabney.
  6. 6:00Silver Lyan (Penn Quarter)One cocktail in the Riggs bank vault — 50 Best №55. Book on Resy; the bar seats take walk-ins early.
  7. 7:30Albi (Navy Yard) ★Michael Rafidi's wood-fired Levantine cooking: Washingtonian's №1 and the first four-star NYT restaurant outside New York. Resy, 30 days out. Backup: The Dabney ★ (Blagden Alley, hearth cooking, Resy) or Maydan (14th St, live fire, easier).
  8. 10:00Service Bar (U Street)Walk-in, no fuss, some of the best drinks in the city (50 Best №23 last year). If it's packed, The Gibson is two blocks away — the original DC speakeasy.

Itinerary

Three days

Downtown and Shaw, then Capitol Hill and the Wharf, then Georgetown and the West End. One quadrant a day.

A long weekend. Day one is the one-day plan; day two crosses to the Hill and the water; day three ends in Georgetown. Book three dinners the morning reservations open: Albi, Rose's Luxury (or Pineapple & Pearls), and Fiola Mare or Xiquet.

Day 1 · Downtown & Shaw

  1. All dayFollow the one-day planYellow, the Mall, Sweet Home Café, Ben's, Blagden Alley, Silver Lyan, Albi, Service Bar.

Day 2 · Capitol Hill & the Wharf

  1. 8:30Eastern Market → Market LunchBlueberry-buckwheat pancakes at the counter, then the flea market outside if it's a weekend. Cash-friendly, walk-in, arrive before nine on Saturday.
  2. 10:30The Capitol & the Library of CongressFree tours; book the Capitol online. The Longworth cafeteria is the visitor's secret for a cheap, decent lunch among staffers.
  3. 1:00Maine Avenue Fish Market (The Wharf)The oldest open-air fish market in America. Jessie Taylor or Captain White's for a half-dozen oysters and a crab cake on the pier. Cash.
  4. 3:0012 Stories (InterContinental, The Wharf)A drink twelve floors up over the Potomac and the Monument. Resy; the terrace fills at golden hour.
  5. 5:30Little Pearl (Capitol Hill) ★A glass of wine and pastries at the café before it turns into a tasting counter. Walk-in by day.
  6. 7:30Rose's Luxury (Barracks Row) ★Aaron Silverman's place, still the most fun starred room in the city. Resy, 30 days out, and the bar takes walk-ins from 5:00. Backup: Pineapple & Pearls ★ next door (Tock; the tasting menu) or Ambar (unlimited Balkan small plates, easier).
  7. 10:00Harold Black or Wisdom (Barracks Row / Capitol Hill)Harold Black is unmarked, Wisdom is all gin. Both a short walk. Or The Tune Inn — a dive since 1947 — for the honest version.

Day 3 · Georgetown & the West End

  1. 9:00Baked & Wired (Georgetown)The cupcakes locals order instead of the ones with the line. Then walk the C&O Canal towpath.
  2. 11:00Georgetown → Dumbarton OaksThe gardens, then M Street. Martin's Tavern for a Bloody Mary in the booth where Kennedy proposed if you need a sit-down.
  3. 1:00Chez Billy Sud (Georgetown)Steak frites in the green room. Resy. Or Emma's Torch two blocks over — a refugee-trained kitchen that just made Resy's Hit List.
  4. 3:30Ching Ching Cha (Georgetown)Tea and dumplings in the tea house. Walk-in.
  5. 6:00Off the Record (Hay-Adams)The basement bar under the hotel across from the White House, where the deals get done. Walk-in; jackets not required but common.
  6. 7:30Fiola Mare (Georgetown Waterfront) or Xiquet (Glover Park) ★Fiola Mare for the seafood and the river; Xiquet for Danny Lledó's Valencian paella tasting. Resy / Tock, 30 days out. Backup: Rasika West End (the palak chaat; OpenTable) or Imperfecto ★ (West End; Resy).
  7. 10:00L'Annexe (Georgetown)The hidden bar under Chez Billy Sud, for one last drink. Or Blues Alley for the late set — jazz since 1965.

Itinerary

Five days

The three-day plan, plus the Ethiopian and Vietnamese neighborhoods, and one day for the splurge.

Enough time to eat like a resident: the outer neighborhoods, a tasting counter, the drag brunch, and a drive to the country. This is a reservations trip. Set alarms for Albi, Rose's, minibar or Jônt, and — if you're driving — the Inn at Little Washington.

Days 1–3

  1. AllFollow the three-day planDowntown, the Hill and the Wharf, Georgetown.

Day 4 · The neighborhoods

  1. 9:00La Tejana (Mount Pleasant)Breakfast tacos from a window — a Bib Gourmand. Walk-in, cash-friendly, gone by noon.
  2. 10:30Mount Pleasant → Columbia HeightsBar del Monte if it's open for lunch (no reservations), Taqueria Habanero for the al pastor, Queen's English for Hong Kong noodles (both Bibs).
  3. 1:00Eden Center (Falls Church; car or Metro to East Falls Church)The Vietnamese mall: pho at Pho Xe Lua, bánh mì at Song Que, a coffee at Huong Binh. Cash at most counters.
  4. 4:00Adams MorganTryst for coffee on the couches, then Jack Rose's terrace for a whiskey from the 2,700-bottle wall.
  5. 6:30Ethiopian on U Street & ShawDukem, Zenebech or Chercher — the largest Ethiopian community outside Africa eats here. Order the veggie combo and a tibs; eat with your hands. Walk-in. Beteseb in Adams Morgan is the new-guard version (JBF semifinalist).
  6. 9:00Green Zone → Death & Co DC (Adams Morgan)Middle Eastern cocktails, then the New York bar's DC outpost. Resy for Death & Co; Green Zone walks in.

Day 5 · The splurge, and Sunday

  1. 11:00Perry's drag brunch (Adams Morgan) or Le Diplomate (Logan Circle)Perry's is the rooftop drag brunch that started DC's tradition (book weeks ahead); Le Diplomate is the brasserie brunch everyone else is at (Resy).
  2. 2:00Union Market (NoMa)The hall: Chai Pani for chaat, Buffalo & Bergen for an egg cream, Bidwell if you want a table. Then La'Shukran for a Levantine afternoon drink.
  3. 4:30Summit (Conrad, CityCenter)The Capitol-dome view, one drink. Resy.
  4. 6:00Barmini (Penn Quarter)José Andrés' cocktail lab, next door to minibar. Tock, reservation only; forty minutes is enough.
  5. 8:00minibar ★★ or Jônt ★★minibar for the José Andrés theater (Tock, weeks out); Jônt for Ryan Ratino's Japanese-leaning counter above Bresca (Tock). The country alternative: The Inn at Little Washington ★★ — ninety minutes' drive, book the room and stay.
  6. 11:00Allegory (Eaton DC)50 Best №57, behind the library door. Resy. Or Off the Record if you'd rather end where the day started.

Gotchas & reminders

Before you go.

The things New Yorkers know and visitors learn the expensive way.

Reservations drop on a schedule.

Most Resy restaurants release 30 days out at 9 or 10am ET; Tock tables (minibar, Jônt, Pineapple & Pearls, Kinship/Métier, Nakazawa) go in blocks — set Notify. Rose's Luxury and Little Pearl hold bar seats for walk-ins; arrive at 5:00 for a 5:30 opening. The Inn at Little Washington is a Resy Global Dining Access restaurant — your Platinum card gets you tables the public can't see.

Cash still matters at the counters.

Ben's Chili Bowl, Horace & Dickie's, the Fish Market, Market Lunch, La Tejana and most of Eden Center are fastest or only in cash. Carry $60 in small bills on the neighborhoods day.

Tip 20%; check for the service charge.

DC's tipped minimum wage has been rising under Initiative 82 and many restaurants now add a 3–20% service or 'I-82' charge — it's disclosed on the menu, and it is not the tip. Read the bill before adding 20% on top. Sales tax on meals is 10%.

The Metro is good, and it closes.

Tap in with a contactless card or phone (SmarTrip is optional now). Trains run until midnight Sunday–Thursday and 1am Friday–Saturday; after that it's a car. Georgetown has no Metro station — walk from Foggy Bottom or take the Circulator.

Security and dress codes.

The Capitol, the White House area and the museums are all bag-check; a small bag saves time. Off the Record, Round Robin and Quill don't enforce dress codes, but this is the most suit-wearing city in America and you'll feel it. Café Milano and the Waldorf expect an effort.

Monday is the dark day.

Most starred restaurants close Sunday and/or Monday — minibar, Jônt, Rose's, Pineapple & Pearls, The Dabney all do. Bars and the Ethiopian restaurants run seven days. Rooftops are seasonal; The Wharf's stay open into November with heaters.

Museums are free; their cafés aren't secret anymore.

Sweet Home Café at NMAAHC and Mitsitam at the American Indian museum are on this list on merit. The museum itself is free, but NMAAHC needs a timed pass — reserve online, or try the 8:15am same-day release.

The two cafeterias.

Anyone can eat at the Longworth House Office Building cafeteria (through security) and the Supreme Court cafeteria when the Court isn't sitting. Cheap, decent, and full of the people who run the country.

Use the Amex credit like a local.

Platinum: $100 per quarter; Gold: $50 twice a year. Pay with the enrolled card at any Resy or Tock venue showing the 'Resy Credit eligible' badge — no booking through Resy required. Check the quarter before the splurge night.

Anything marked verify, verify.

DC lost a record ~100 restaurants in 2025 and the churn continued into 2026 — Cranes, Reverie and Tail Up Goat among them. Thirty seconds on Resy or Google before you cross town.

Half-smoke, mumbo sauce, jumbo slice.

The half-smoke is a spiced pork-and-beef sausage — order it 'all the way' (chili, mustard, onions). Mumbo sauce is DC's sweet-tangy wing sauce, born in the carry-outs. The jumbo slice is an Adams Morgan 2am institution; it is not good, and that's not the point.

One for the road.

Union Station has Pearl's Bagels and a Blue Bottle; National airport has a Ben's Chili Bowl. Dulles has nothing on this list — eat before you go.
